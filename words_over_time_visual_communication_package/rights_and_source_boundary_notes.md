# Rights and source-boundary notes

## Article position

Words Over Time should be presented as a visual communication system that keeps source and rights boundaries visible. Rights information is not only legal housekeeping; it changes what the interface can responsibly show.

## Source-boundary principles

1. Frequency is source-specific visibility.
2. Attestation is source evidence, not proof of first historical use.
3. Context snippets are local evidence, not total semantic explanation.
4. Variant grouping is editorial policy, not automatic definition.
5. Missing or restricted evidence is a boundary condition, not proof of non-existence.
6. Rights and attribution shape what can be displayed, quoted, reproduced, or redistributed.

## Rights-aware visual communication

The project should not present restricted source material as if it were open corpus data. The interface can cite, summarize, or point to source records without republishing full restricted text.

The article can describe how the interface uses rights labels and source ledgers to make publication constraints visible to readers. That is part of the methodological contribution.

## Manuscript wording

Use:

> Rights and attribution are treated as part of the evidence architecture because they determine what can be inspected, quoted, reproduced, or withheld.

Use:

> The boundary column does not weaken the visual claim; it defines the conditions under which the visual claim remains responsible.

Avoid:

> The website owns or releases all source material.

Avoid:

> Corpus absence proves historical absence.

## Suggested limitations paragraph

This article analyzes Words Over Time as a designed visual communication artifact. It does not test reader comprehension, measure usability, or claim that the interface empirically improves interpretation. Its contribution is analytic and methodological: it shows how source boundaries, evidence types, uncertainty, and rights constraints can be encoded as visible interface structure.
