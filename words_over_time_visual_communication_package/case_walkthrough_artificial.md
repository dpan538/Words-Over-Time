# Case walkthrough: artificial

## Article role

`artificial` is the strongest case for showing that semantic-family visualization is not the same as dictionary definition.

Use this case to explain how Words Over Time keeps adjacent meanings visible without flattening them into synonym equivalence: made, synthetic, simulated, realistic, fake-adjacent, and human-boundary meanings remain related but not identical.

## Visual sequence

The page presents six movements:

1. Artificial before fake: a spatial reading of word family, technical construction, and sense boundaries.
2. Artificial under pressure: a hover-state diagram that reveals selected readings.
3. Mechanical reproduction: apparatus layers, manufactured senses, and authenticity pressure.
4. Suspicion orbit: recurrent contexts where artificial gathers negative charge.
5. Semantic attractor: a field relation among made, synthetic, simulated, realistic, and fake-adjacent meanings.
6. Human boundary: a layered stack moving from apparatus to support, replacement, reproduction, voice, cognition, and human process.

## Source-boundary analysis

The semantic chamber and attractor are Variant components. They show relations among meanings without treating those relations as an automatic dictionary definition.

The mechanical reproduction suite is a Context component. It gives local conditions for semantic pressure, not a single causal explanation.

The suspicion orbit is a Boundary component. It marks negative charge as historically recurring context rather than the essence of the word.

The human boundary chart is a Boundary and Context component. It visualizes movement across human/process boundaries but does not claim a simple replacement narrative.

## Claim discipline

Use:

> The `artificial` page treats semantic proximity as a designed relation that must remain visually distinct from synonymy, definition, and proof.

Avoid:

> The page shows that artificial means fake.
