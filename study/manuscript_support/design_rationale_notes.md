# Design rationale notes

## Article framing

Words Over Time should be framed as visual communication research, not as a website showcase.

The central claim is that visual communication can make semantic change legible while preserving distinctions between frequency, attestation, context, interpretation, source limits, and rights.

## Design method

The evidence grid separates six roles:

- Signal: source-specific frequency or visibility.
- Attestation: lexical or earliest-known-use evidence.
- Variant: form, phrase, spelling, and grouping policy.
- Context: snippets, examples, and domain evidence.
- Boundary: explicit claim limits and uncertainty.
- Rights: attribution and reuse constraints.

The grid is not decorative. It is a method for making both evidence and limitation visible.

## Expected study contribution

The reader study should test whether the evidence grid reduces overclaiming from frequency and improves source-boundary awareness.

The study does not need to prove a universal interface effect. Its value is to document how readers interpret a public-facing humanities interface that treats claim boundaries as part of the visual system.
