# Reader task script

## Introduction for participant

You will see materials about three words: `data`, `artificial`, and `forever`.

Some materials are simplified, frequency-first versions. Others show a fuller evidence grid with separate areas for frequency signal, attestation, variant policy, context, boundaries, and rights.

Please answer based only on what the material allows you to infer.

## Core tasks

1. Which part of the material supports an earliest attestation or earliest known-use claim?
2. Does a frequency increase prove that the word became more culturally important? Explain briefly.
3. Can this material prove the first historical use of the word in all English usage?
4. Which part of the material tells you what the page cannot claim?
5. What source boundary limits the chart interpretation?
6. Is the semantic group shown here an automatic dictionary definition?
7. If a piece of evidence is missing, does that prove the usage did not exist?
8. Explain the word's change in one sentence using at least two evidence types.
9. Which evidence type did you trust most, and which felt most uncertain?
10. What visual structure most helped or blocked your understanding?

## Optional think-aloud prompt

As you answer, you may describe what you are looking at and why you trust or distrust it. Please avoid sharing personally identifying information.
